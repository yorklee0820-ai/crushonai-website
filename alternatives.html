CrushOn AI already offers voice playback. If audio is your reason for leaving, compare the interaction you want, such as hands-free calls, rather than assuming any service with a voice feature is an upgrade.

1. Character.AI: for character chat with firm content limits

Character.AI belongs on your list if you enjoy fictional conversations but don’t need explicit roleplay. Its content policy prohibits pornographic content. That boundary matters more than any comparison of interface design: if it rules out the stories you want to write, a paid plan won’t solve the mismatch.

There is still plenty to compare. Character.AI offers voice features and tools for preserving conversation details. Its current plans distinguish between models and memory tools, so compare the tier you would actually use instead of judging the whole service from one free conversation.

Start with ordinary free conversations and pay attention to whether the character keeps its personality through a change of topic. A larger feature list tells you little about whether you like the replies. Our CrushOn AI vs Character.AI comparison covers the broader differences.

2. Janitor AI: for character creators and branching stories

Janitor AI is worth a look if your favorite part is building fictional worlds. The publisher’s official app listing documents character creation and editing, scripts, lorebooks, and public conversations you can fork. Those are concrete tools for developing a setting or taking an existing scene in another direction.

For that kind of user, the attraction is creative control. A lorebook can hold background for a world; a fork lets you explore an alternative path without treating every conversation as a fresh start. Whether the resulting writing suits you still depends on the character and model.

The app is free to download with optional Janitor+ purchases. Check which features require payment on the version you use. Do not rely on older guides that present an external API key as the only way to begin. Read our CrushOn AI vs Janitor AI comparison alongside the current official listing.

3. Candy AI: for chat built around a visual companion

Candy AI is worth considering if you want a companion you can picture as well as chat with. Images and voice notes are part of its offering, but they also change the economics. How much you generate matters as much as which plan you choose.

The important detail is how you pay. Candy’s terms separate subscription access to messaging from tokens used for extras such as images and voice notes. Free access is limited, and a subscription does not make every feature unlimited.

Before subscribing, work out how often you would request media. Someone who mostly types may have a very different monthly bill from someone who generates images throughout each conversation. Check the included allowance and token prices at checkout, not just the advertised subscription figure. See the Candy AI comparison for more context.

4. Nomi AI: for managing an ongoing character history

Nomi offers a concrete reason to investigate its memory system: you can work with some of the information it keeps. Mind Map 2.0 provides a visual graph and searchable table, with options to find, prioritize, edit and add entries. That is useful if you want to maintain an ongoing story without repeatedly pasting the same background into chat.

There are limits. Nomi’s own memory guide explains that storing a detail does not guarantee retrieving it at the right moment. Calling it the best memory system would require comparative testing that feature documentation cannot provide.

Try it with a few harmless fictional details, then return later and see what survives naturally. The ability to correct a Mind Map entry may matter more to you than a large advertised capacity. Our Nomi AI comparison explores that choice.

5. Kindroid: for voice and detailed character controls

Kindroid is a sensible shortlist option if you want to talk aloud as well as type. Its documented features include voice calls, video calls and screen sharing. Check what you want from video, though: letting a companion see your camera or screen is a different experience from watching a generated character clip.

Its writing controls are also worth attention. The model guide separates backstory, journal entries, response directives and example messages. You can use those fields to define identity, preserve lore and guide tone instead of putting every instruction into one long introduction.

That flexibility suits someone willing to tune a character. You may not need it if you prefer a ready-made conversation. Also check media allowances: voice and image features can involve quotas or additional credits. Don’t assume a paid plan removes every limit.

6. Nectar AI: for building a character and adjusting the output

Nectar AI gives character creators several specific controls. According to its official FAQ, you can adjust appearance, personality, backstory and speaking style, choose among models, and change response length. Image and video tools sit alongside chat.

Response length is worth trying early. If you want short exchanges, a platform that keeps producing paragraphs can be frustrating even when the writing is coherent. If you prefer collaborative storytelling, check whether longer responses leave enough room for your next turn.

Nectar also says older exchanges may fade from context; paid extended memory is not a promise of perfect recall. Its limited free access can help you judge the basic fit, while premium actions use credits. Compare those allowances with your habits before assuming it costs the same as CrushOn AI.

7. Lovescape: for creating a companion across chat and images

Lovescape is another option for readers who want a defined companion with both conversation and visual features. Its premium membership guide lists unlimited chat, voice messages and character-creation options. That combination is worth exploring if choosing traits and developing a recognizable character are part of the appeal.

Keep an eye on the separate currency. Lovescape calls it Chips, and its help page says image-generation costs are displayed before a request. Chips do not expire, but purchasing them requires an active subscription.

Ask whether you’ll use the voice and image tools enough to justify their combined cost. Unlimited chat can still sit beside a metered media budget. Check both before committing.

How to choose a CrushOn AI alternative

Narrow the list to two services with the feature you actually miss. Then give both the same short character brief and opening scene. A friendly first reply is easy to like; a conversation that still works after you redirect it tells you more.

  • Check the writing. Change the subject, ask for a shorter reply and correct one invented detail. Notice whether the character adapts without losing its voice or repeating the same phrases.
  • Check continuity. Introduce three fictional facts, continue the scene, and return another day. Look at what the service remembers and how easily you can fix mistakes.
  • Check the whole bill. Separate the subscription, media credits and any external model charges. An annual plan’s monthly equivalent is not the amount charged upfront.
  • Check privacy before getting personal. Read the current policy on stored conversations, deletion and data use. Content freedom does not tell you how private a service is.

Nomi explains that chats are not end-to-end encrypted because its service processes them. Candy’s privacy policy permits anonymized interactions to be used for training. Those details matter more than a general promise of private chat. Use fictional information while you decide whether a service earns your trust.

If you already enjoy CrushOn AI, switch only when another service solves a problem you can name. Better controls, a preferred voice experience or useful media tools can justify the move. A longer feature list alone is a poor reason to rebuild a character you like.
